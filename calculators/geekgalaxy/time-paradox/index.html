The Rules of Temporal Mechanics

Time travel is messy. If you go back and change something, you risk breaking causality.

The Major Theories

  1. The Grandfather Paradox: You go back and kill your grandfather. So you are never born. So you can't go back to kill him. So he lives. So you are born...
  2. * *Result*: The universe crashes (or you just fail).
  3. The Multiverse (Avengers/DBZ): Going back creates a *new* timeline. You can kill your grandpa, but you just return to a different future. Your original timeline is unchanged.
  4. The Bootstrap Paradox: You go back and give Shakespeare his own plays. He publishes them. Who wrote them? The information has no origin.
  5. Frequency Sensitivity (Back to the Future): Time is like a river. You can throw a stone (save your parents' marriage) and the ripples change things, but the river flows on.

The Fine Print (FAQ)

Can I kill Hitler?
Most travelers try. Usually, the timeline fights back (gun jams, he moves), or a worse dictator replaces him. This is 'Time's Defense Mechanism'.
What is a Time Remnant?
A version of you from a deleted timeline that managed to survive in the speed force/void. Avoid them.
Is time travel possible?
Forward? Yes, via relativity (go fast). Backward? Physics says 'probably not', as it violates entropy.
